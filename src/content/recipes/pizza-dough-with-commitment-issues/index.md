### A Dough That Won't Settle Down

This dough proofs perfectly, stretches like a dream, and then, without fail, springs back the moment you try to shape it. It's not being difficult — it's just processing.

> [!NOTE]
> Room temperature matters more than the recipe lets on. A cold kitchen means a dough that takes its time deciding anything.

---

### Ingredients, Slightly Indecisive

| Ingredient       | Amount | Mood               |
| ---------------- | ------ | ------------------ |
| Bread flour      | 500 g  | Steady             |
| Warm water       | 325 ml | Reassuring         |
| Active dry yeast | 7 g    | Optimistic         |
| Salt             | 10 g   | Quietly in control |
| Olive oil        | 2 tbsp | Smooth-talking     |

---

### Method, Taken One Step at a Time

- [ ] Dissolve yeast in warm water and wait for it to believe in itself (5–10 min)
- [ ] Mix in flour and salt until a shaggy dough forms
- [ ] Knead for 10 minutes, or until the dough stops arguing back
- [ ] First rise: 1–2 hours, covered, somewhere warm
- [ ] Punch down, divide, and let rest 20 minutes before shaping
- [ ] Shape — gently, and without taking it personally when it springs back

> [!TIP]
> If the dough keeps shrinking back while you stretch it, walk away for five minutes. This is not a metaphor. It genuinely just needs the break.

---

### Final Thoughts

The crust came out chewy in the middle and crisp at the edges — proof that indecision, given enough time, can still turn into something with structure.

Overall rating: **8 out of 10 unresolved shapes**.
