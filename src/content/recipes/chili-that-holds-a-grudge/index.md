### A Simmer With a Long Memory for Heat

This chili starts out mild-mannered. By hour two, it remembers every chili it's ever been compared to and decides to settle the score.

> [!CAUTION]
> The heat builds gradually and then all at once. Taste at the one-hour mark, not just at the start, or you will be caught off guard.

---

### The Spice Lineup

| Spice          | Amount  | Role                          |
| -------------- | ------- | ----------------------------- |
| Chili powder   | 3 tbsp  | The instigator                |
| Cumin          | 1 tbsp  | Keeps things grounded         |
| Smoked paprika | 1 tbsp  | Adds a long memory            |
| Cayenne        | ½–1 tsp | Escalates the situation       |
| Bay leaves     | 2       | Watches quietly from the side |

---

### Prep Checklist

- [ ] Brown the beef (or lentils, if the chili is feeling forgiving) over medium-high heat
- [ ] Sauté onion, garlic, and bell pepper until softened
- [ ] Stir in spices and toast for one minute, until fragrant and slightly smug
- [ ] Add tomatoes, beans, and broth; bring to a simmer
- [ ] Simmer uncovered for at least 90 minutes, stirring occasionally
- [ ] Taste at the one-hour mark and adjust cayenne accordingly

> [!NOTE]
> A square of dark chocolate stirred in during the last 20 minutes rounds out the heat without anyone noticing it's there.

---

### Final Thoughts

Deep, smoky, and unwilling to let anything go — in the best way. Leftovers are, unsurprisingly, even better the next day, once the chili's had more time to think about it.

Overall rating: **9 out of 10 long-held grudges**.
